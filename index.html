About the Lab

The Collective Memory and Dynamic Cognition Lab investigates how memory is shaped and reshaped as people navigate dynamic contexts, including (but not limited to) social interaction and group recall. We combine behavioral experiments with quantitative and computational methods to study how memory changes over time, how it is constructed and revised individually and in groups, and how those processes relate to the spread and correction of misinformation. Undergraduate research assistants are core members of the lab, contributing to every stage of the research process, from study design to data collection and analysis.

Research

We study how memory is shaped and reshaped as people navigate dynamic contexts, combining behavioral experiments with quantitative and computational methods.

Work in Progress
  • Context Shift Mechanisms. How does a stark context shift, induced by directed forgetting instructions, interfere with context reinstatement? We're examining this question at multiple levels of analysis, including potential breakdowns in temporal context reconstruction using fine-grained analyses (e.g., Lag-CRP). Phase: Data Collection
  • Cue Strategy as Support or Hindrance. How does the serial position of words at encoding influence the extent to which they support or hinder recall when used as cues during retrieval? We're addressing this question by integrating classic work on part-set cuing effects with modern theoretical ideas about context maintenance and retrieval. Phase: Data Collection
  • Quantifying Collective Memory in Larger Groups. A strict definition of collective memory requires that all members of a group recall a particular word or detail. In real life, that bar is too high. We are developing metrics for characterizing collective memory in larger groups and networks that take a spectrum approach instead of an all-or-nothing one. Phase: In Development

Themes

Dynamic Cognition

Memory and cognition are not static snapshots; they shift with context, time, and experience. We examine how remembering, learning, and belief change unfold dynamically as people navigate new information and changing circumstances.

Collaborative & Collective Memory

Social interaction is one powerful context that shapes memory. How do groups remember together, and what emerges from that process: shared understanding, converging error, or both? We study how interaction shapes what individuals and groups come to know and recall.

Misinformation & Knowledge Integrity

How does false information take hold in individual and collective memory, and what conditions make groups more or less resistant to it? This work connects basic memory research to real-world questions about trust and shared knowledge.
